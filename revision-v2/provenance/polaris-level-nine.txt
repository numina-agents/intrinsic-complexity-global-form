# Level-nine boundary for the full-center PSU(3) real-descent family

Polaris, 14 November 2026. Provisional complete argument for Titania's written
independent check. This is a component of the ONE authorized P25 revision,
not a separate paper. Submitted v1 and reports are unchanged.

## Statement

Let C = C(sl_3,9) be the standard unitary category, J = 9 Lambda_1,
A = 1 + J + J^2 its regular Tannakian algebra, and D = C_A^0.
Then D has no simple-fixing conjugate-linear strong monoidal
autoequivalence. Therefore it has no split real fusion form and no gauge
with every associator matrix real, even under nonunitary changes of bases.
It nevertheless has a nonsplit real form, supplied by descended quantum bar.

## The useful invariant is the Omega stabilizer, not the abstract full group

Put Omega = F(Lambda_1 + Lambda_2), F = A tensor -. It is simple and local,
with categorical twist exp(2 pi i * 3/12) = i. The unique stationary parent
weight U = 3 Lambda_1 + 3 Lambda_2 resolves into U_0,U_1,U_2.
The split parent bar descends as in P25 v1, Lemma 3.1, to a coherent
reverse-braided conjugate-linear involution T. It fixes EVERY free-orbit
simple, hence fixes Omega, and transposes exactly two U_j.

Edie-Michell 2102.09065v4, Lemma 3.8, bounds the braided Omega stabilizer
of the adjoint de-equivariantization by D_3. Its possible labelled actions
are generated by

    R: (X,chi_j) -> (X,chi_{j+1}),
    C: (X,chi_j) -> (X^*,chi_{-j}).

This is an UPPER bound, so no extra realization or coherence assertion
about these six candidates is needed here. The lemma DOES apply at level
9; the small excluded sl_3 level is 3, not 9. The proof was read ENTIRELY,
including the character-idempotent calculation and its upper-bound scope.
The precise category identification is Lemma 2.20 together with trivial
universal grading (Lemma 2.18 / Corollary 2.19, parameters r=2,
m=m'=3, m''=1).

The neutral free orbit labels P = F(3 Lambda_1), Q = F(3 Lambda_2)
are different. Their affine rotation orbits are

    P: {(6,3,0),(0,6,3),(3,0,6)},
    Q: {(6,0,3),(3,6,0),(0,3,6)}.

Thus every candidate R^a C exchanges P and Q. Each R^a acts on the
resolved triple by identity or a 3-cycle. None of the six candidates has
T's permutation, because T fixes P,Q and acts as a transposition on the
resolved triple.

Even though EqBr(D) = S_4 (Edie-Michell, Theorem 1.4 / Lemma 5.3),
ANY hypothetical braided equivalence with T's permutation fixes Omega
and therefore lies in the just-excluded stabilizer. No computation of
the exceptional generators is necessary. In particular abstract S_4
does not mean that an isolated resolution transposition is realized.

## Remaining intrinsic hypotheses and proof

Schopieray 1608.03155v2, Theorem 1 in section 3.5, proves simplicity of
D for every k=3m, m>=2; at k=9 this gives primeness. Edie-Michell,
Lemma 2.18 and Corollary 2.19, give Inv(D)=1 and U(D)=1, so the
inherited spherical pivotal structure is unique. Nikshych
1801.06125v2, Corollary 4.8, then gives exactly the given braiding
and its reverse on the fixed tensor category.

If K were a simple-fixing conjugate-linear strong monoidal equivalence,
uniqueness of the pivotal structure and Omega's nonreal twist force K
to reverse braiding, exactly as in P25 v1, Proposition 2.1. Then KT is
complex-linear braided and its simple permutation equals that of T.
The stabilizer argument excludes it. This proof needs no square or
unitarity assumption on K. Real-gauge and split-real-form implications
are the coefficient-conjugation implications proved in P25 v1.

## Consequence for the family presentation

The same Omega-stabilizer proof applies uniformly to every k>=9 divisible
by 3. The two displayed free orbits with 6 replaced by k-3 are distinct
precisely when k>6 (given positive k divisible by 3). The adjoint twist
is exp(2 pi i *3/(k+3)), nonreal for all k>=6. Thus the exceptional
level 9 can be INCLUDED in a single negative family argument. Levels
3 and 6 still require actual positive descent; this note does not
construct it. At k=6 the two free dual orbits COINCIDE, so the argument
correctly loses its separating pair.
