Referee report on P25 v1, “A connected Chern–Simons theory with inherently complex associators”

Verdict: **correct**. Theorem 1.1 is proved: the specified full-center local category of the standard unitary SU(3) category at level 12 has no simple-fixing conjugate-linear strong monoidal autoequivalence. Consequently it has no split real fusion form and no associator gauge with all entries real, including gauges obtained by nonunitary changes of fusion bases. I found no mathematical error or unresolved proof gap in this version. No correction is required for correctness.

I checked the complete submitted TeX, including the general obstruction, the real-algebra and local-module transport lemma, its fixed-point argument, the small-level remark, and every hypothesis in the application. The following records the substantive checks and their scope.

1. Proposition 2.1 (paper.tex lines 116–162).

The transported-braiding formula has the correct tensorator directions: it is a morphism KX tensor KY to KY tensor KX. The hexagon identities transport through a conjugate-linear strong monoidal equivalence because their compositions and tensor products are preserved. Transporting the pivotal structure through the canonical comparisons of duals gives another pivotal structure on the same category, so uniqueness identifies it with p. The resulting twist identity is valid also for a nonunitary K. On a simple object, applying K conjugates its twist scalar. If K fixes all simple isomorphism classes and preserves c, naturality of the twist then forces every twist scalar to be real. The nonreal-twist hypothesis excludes that possibility, leaving the reverse braiding.

I checked the composition step with the tensorators of KT: conjugation of scalars occurs twice, and transport of braiding commutes with inversion and interchanging its two objects. Thus two conjugate-linear reverse-braided equivalences compose to a complex-linear braided equivalence. Since K fixes every simple class, its composition with T has precisely T's simple permutation. This contradicts the permutation-image hypothesis, without assuming K has a coherent square or preserves a dagger structure.

A split real form does supply a simple-fixing semilinear equivalence after complexification. Likewise, an all-real associator presentation supplies such an equivalence by conjugating coefficients on a simple-labelled skeleton. This construction needs the associativity and unit constraints to be respected; it does not need the chosen evaluation or pivotal matrices to be real. Thus the final implications, including the exclusion of nonunitary real gauges, follow.

The auxiliary uniqueness claim at lines 164–171 also checks: the ratio of two pivotal structures is a monoidal natural automorphism of the identity; its simple scalars obey a_X a_Y = a_Z whenever Z is a constituent of X tensor Y, and hence factor through the universal grading group.

2. Lemma 3.1 (lines 181–254).

On the isotropic invertible subgroup H the braiding is symmetric, so a reverse-braided parent datum becomes braided on this restriction. The split real descent has one-dimensional real fusion spaces. Its diagonal abelian-cocycle trace is one: in the unitary pointed restriction the diagonal braiding is the canonical twist, which is trivial on an isotropic subgroup. The cited Eilenberg–Mac Lane theorem permits arbitrary abelian coefficient groups, so applying it to the multiplicative group R^times really trivializes the cocycle over R, rather than merely over C. Transport of the graded real group algebra therefore produces the required connected commutative separable real algebra. Its separability can also be seen directly by averaging over H with coefficient 1/|H|. Its complexification is the regular Tannakian algebra. The resulting coherent identification u has the stated square identity.

The module-action formula has source A_H tensor Phi(M) and target Phi(M), and is the usual transported algebra action. Under braiding reversal the A_H–M monodromy becomes its inverse, conjugated by the tensorators. The equation r M = r is equivalent to r M^{-1} = r. Furthermore r c_{M,A_H} = r c_{A_H,M}^{-1} follows by composing the locality equation with c_{A_H,M}^{-1}. This identifies the transported right action with the right action defining the ordinary local relative tensor product. Consequently the parent tensorator descends through its balancing quotient and remains invertible. The pentagon, unit, and square coherence descend through these epimorphic quotient maps. Applying the same calculation to the defining quotient equation for the local braiding gives exactly its reverse, with the objects interchanged in the inverse. Thus the constructed T is a coherent reverse-braided tensor involution on local modules.

For the resolution argument I checked both the twisted-group-algebra identification and its grading. A coherent real frame on U, together with u, gives a coherent frame on the free module. The induced conjugation on its endomorphism algebra preserves each stabilizer degree: the parent fixes h and U, and u cannot mix distinct invertible summands of A_H. Each degree therefore has a one-dimensional fixed real subspace. Nonzero homogeneous elements are invertible. For a cyclic stabilizer of odd order r, a real homogeneous generator satisfies x^r = a with a nonzero real scalar. A real r-th root rescales it to x^r = 1, giving R[C_r]. Its complex primitive idempotents consist of one fixed idempotent and (r−1)/2 conjugate pairs. Since the complex endomorphism algebra is C^r, the corresponding induced simple summands are distinct and have multiplicity one. This proves the claimed simple permutation; for r=3 it is a transposition.

This argument also proves the stated existence of a nonsplit real form. It does not alone exclude a different split local datum, and the paper correctly supplies an additional intrinsic obstruction.

3. Section 4 and the proof of Theorem 1.1 (lines 270–355).

I recalculated the SU(3) conformal weight as

    h(a,b) = (a^2 + ab + b^2 + 3a + 3b)/(3(k+3)).

At k=12 the center generator has order three and h(12,0)=4, so its twist is one. Balancing gives its monodromy with (a,b) as exp(−2*pi*i*(a+2b)/3), verifying the neutral-weight condition. Cyclic affine rotation has exactly the fixed triple (4,4,4). Direct enumeration reproduces every entry of the row-count list, its sum 31, the ten free orbits, and three resolutions, hence rank 13. A nontrivial Deligne factorization of a nondegenerate category would factor its rank into integers greater than one; rank 13 therefore proves primeness.

The source parameters are r=2, k=12, m=m'=3, m''=1. The Tannakian divisibility condition holds, the small exclusions of the invertible-object lemma do not apply, and (r+1)/(m*m'')=1. Thus the cited formulas give both trivial invertible group and trivial universal grading. With the inherited spherical structure, this proves pivotal uniqueness. Nikshych's result then supplies exactly c^A and its reverse on the fixed underlying fusion category, rather than merely identifying a selected pair of quantum braidings up to arbitrary relabelling.

The weight (1,1) is neutral and has trivial stabilizer, so its induction Omega is simple and local. Twist descent and the conformal-weight formula give h(1,1)=1/5, hence the nonreal categorical twist exp(2*pi*i/5). No central-charge shift enters this computation.

The Galindo datum fixes parent simple classes and reverses the braiding for the same tensorator. Induction intertwines this datum with the constructed local datum; consequently every free-orbit simple is fixed. The fixed-point calculation above exchanges precisely two of the three resolutions. Therefore T's permutation really is an isolated transposition on 13 labels.

For the whole braided group, Theorem 1.4 of Edie-Michell applies, with p=t=0 and no exceptional level-12 case; it gives D_3 of order six. Lemmas 3.8–3.9 give the stated character-shift and charge-conjugation actions. These six classes are all realized. The two displayed affine orbits for 3*Lambda_1 and 3*Lambda_2 are disjoint and are exchanged by every reflection, while rotations fix all free-orbit labels. Thus no element of the entire braided group has the required isolated transposition. This verifies the final hypothesis of Proposition 2.1 and proves the main theorem.

As additional independent checks, exact dimension arithmetic shows that only the unit has dimension one and that Omega has a unique simple dimension. Since pivotal structures are unique, every braided autoequivalence preserves these dimensions and fixes Omega. Thus the applicable Omega-stabilizer calculation in Edie-Michell's Lemmas 3.8–3.9 independently checks that the paper has not omitted additional braided permutations. The rank argument and the group argument concern the stated local category and impose no assumption that a hypothetical real datum be induced from its parent.
